# axiom: evaluation report

This report describes what the proof of concept builds, what a reader must trust, what the measurements show and where the approach stops. Each number is followed by its source: a row of a file under `bench/`, written as `file: case / kind` or `file: section / item`, or the name of a test. `make bench` regenerates `results.csv` and `comparison.csv`, `make coverage` regenerates `coverage.csv`, and `make demo` prints the end-to-end table.

## Architecture

Alice describes a probabilistic system as a model in the `.axm` language. The engine, written in Rust, analyses the model and writes a certificate: a JSON file holding the model, each claim and the numbers needed to verify it. Bob runs the checker, written in Lean 4, on the certificate. The checker accepts or rejects each claim. Bob neither reruns nor reads the engine.

The Rust workspace in `crates/` is untrusted throughout.

| Crate | Role |
|---|---|
| `axiom-core` | Exact rational probabilities, transition rows and matrices, the two-state chain and its affine update, and the Hoeffding bound. `kernel.rs` holds the functions translated for proof 3. |
| `axiom-model` | Lexer, parser and static checker for `.axm` files. |
| `axiom-cert` | Writes and reads certificates in format versions 1 to 4. |
| `axiom-sim` | Seeded sampling of chains and retries, and Hoeffding estimates. |
| `axiom-infer` | Black-box analysis: runs a model it cannot see and proposes candidate claims from samples. |
| `axiom-cli` | The `axiom` command: `analyse` writes a certificate, `check` runs the Lean checker on it. |
| `axiom-wasm` | The engine compiled to WebAssembly for the web interface in `ui/`. |
| `axiom-bench` | The benchmark harness (`axiom-bench`) and the coverage report (`axiom-coverage`). |

The Lean side has two projects. `lean/` is the checker, `AxiomCheck`. `AxiomCheck.Parse` reads the JSON, `AxiomCheck.Semantics` states what a model and each claim mean, `AxiomCheck.Check` holds `check : Certificate → Bool` and its soundness theorem `check_sound`, and `AxiomCheck.Theorems` proves the mathematics behind each claim kind, such as the bound for every step that follows from a fixed point and a contraction factor below one. `lean-engine/` holds proof 3: the Lean translation of `axiom_core::kernel`, generated by Charon and Aeneas at the versions pinned in `proof3.toml`, and the correctness theorems in `AxiomEngine/Correctness.lean`.

Three proofs remove three different doubts. Proof 1 concerns the model: Lean proves properties of the model itself. Proof 2 concerns each certificate: by `check_sound`, an accepted claim is true of the model. Proof 3 concerns the engine: the translated kernel functions compute what the semantics says, for every input. Proof 2 does not depend on proof 3. Proof 3 narrows the search for the cause when a true claim is rejected.

## Trusted computing base per milestone

The trusted computing base (TCB) is what must be correct for an accepted claim to be true.

| Milestone | Adds | TCB after the milestone |
|---|---|---|
| M0, workspace and decisions | the contracts later milestones refer to | nothing yet |
| M1, model language and the link chain in Rust | the engine | nothing: the Rust side is never trusted |
| M2, Lean ingestion and Mass checking | the checker | Lean and mathlib, the parser `AxiomCheck.Parse`, the meaning of a model and of each claim in `AxiomCheck.Semantics`, and the statement of the soundness theorem |
| M3, fixed point, contraction and convergence | three claim kinds | as M2, plus the statement of the key theorem (a fixed point and a contraction imply the bound for every step) |
| M4, parametric stability and termination | two claim kinds | as M3, plus the statement of the least-fixed-point semantics for recursion |
| M5, Estimated certainty | the Estimate kind | as M4, for the arithmetic of the bound only; the sampling, the random number generator and the independence of runs are outside the TCB, and every Estimate says so |
| M6, black-box analysis | `axiom-infer` | unchanged; black-box output stays Estimated unless a candidate is then proven through M3 or M4 |
| M7, engine proofs (proof 3) | the translated kernel and its theorems | as M6, plus the pinned Charon and Aeneas, the statement of each covered function's correctness, and the model of `ExactRational` as Lean's `Rat`, for the covered functions only |
| M8, evaluation | measurement only | unchanged |

Both Lean projects are audited at the kernel level. `make lean-audit` and `make engine-check` fail if any declaration depends on an axiom other than `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` (`coverage.csv: project / lean` and `project / lean-engine`). The audits reject `sorryAx` and `Lean.ofReduceBool`, so `native_decide` is outside the TCB.

### Checking against simulating

The central question was whether checking a claim in Lean costs much less than simulating to the same confidence. `make bench` compared the wall-clock time of `axiom check` on the whole certificate with the time to simulate the claim's conclusion to tolerance 1/1000 (or the bound's own tolerance) with failure probability 1/1000, using Hoeffding's sample count. The hypothesis was counted as confirmed for a kind when checking was at least 10 times cheaper on every fixture with a claim of that kind.

| Kind | Least ratio on the fixtures | Source row | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass | 0.023 | `comparison.csv: periodic / Mass` | refuted |
| FixedPoint | 0.674 | `comparison.csv: links / FixedPoint` | refuted |
| Contraction | 0.163 | `comparison.csv: links / Contraction` | refuted |
| ConvergenceBound | 0.187 | `comparison.csv: links / ConvergenceBound` | refuted |
| Termination | 0.063 | `comparison.csv: retry / Termination` | refuted |
| ParametricContraction | at least 19.410 | `comparison.csv: links-param / ParametricContraction` | confirmed |

On the fixtures, checking was much cheaper only for ParametricContraction. The ratio there is a lower bound. The simulation covered a 10 by 10 grid of parameters with 3,317,620,000 samples, while the checked claim holds for every parameter in the intervals, which no finite simulation reaches (`comparison.csv: links-param / ParametricContraction`).

For the other kinds, a check cost between 853.4 ms (`comparison.csv: horizon-200 / Mass`) and 1265.1 ms (`comparison.csv: horizon-100 / Mass`), while simulating a small fixture to tolerance 1/1000 took tens to hundreds of milliseconds. Simulating the 13-step bound for the link chain, for example, took 173.9 ms against a check of 929.7 ms (`comparison.csv: links / ConvergenceBound`).

The scaling family showed the conditions under which checking was cheaper.

- **Tighter tolerances.** Simulation needs Hoeffding's sample count, which grows with the inverse square of the tolerance. For the link chain's ConvergenceBound the ratio is 0.001 at tolerance 1/100 (`comparison.csv: tolerance-2 / ConvergenceBound`), 0.196 at 1/1000 (`tolerance-3 / ConvergenceBound`), 27.272 at 1/10,000 (`tolerance-4 / ConvergenceBound`), 3041.202 at 1/100,000 (`tolerance-5 / ConvergenceBound`) and 409,223.597 at 1/1,000,000 (`tolerance-6 / ConvergenceBound`). The check time stayed between 869.4 ms and 933.1 ms across these rows.
- **Slow mixing.** As the contraction factor approaches one, simulation must run more steps to reach the steady state. The FixedPoint ratio rose from 0.916 at factor 3/5 (`comparison.csv: horizon-5 / FixedPoint`) to 20.197 at factor 99/100 (`horizon-200 / FixedPoint`), and the ConvergenceBound ratio from 0.207 (`horizon-5 / ConvergenceBound`) to 4.810 (`horizon-200 / ConvergenceBound`).
- **More states had little effect.** For Mass the ratio grew from 0.133 with 3 states (`comparison.csv: states-3 / Mass`) to 1.137 with 16 (`states-16 / Mass`), still short of 10.

Contraction was refuted on every row, from 0.072 (`comparison.csv: horizon-100 / Contraction`) to 0.186 (`templates/slow-repair / Contraction`). Most of each check's time was the fixed cost of starting the Lean checker. `results.csv` records it per case in `check_ms`. The ratios describe this checker as built, not a checker with a lower start-up cost.

Rows with `sim_extrapolated = true` in `comparison.csv` were scaled linearly from a prefix of the samples. The tolerance-5 and tolerance-6 rows are among them.

### Verification coverage

`make coverage` reads the Lean and Rust sources and writes `bench/coverage.md` and `bench/coverage.csv`. It fails if any `sorry` appears outside a comment, which the test `a_sorry_outside_comments_fails_generation` in `crates/axiom-bench/tests/coverage.rs` checks.

- All 7 claim kinds have a proved soundness theorem: Mass (`checkRow_sound`), FixedPoint (`checkFixedPoint_sound`), Contraction (`checkContraction_sound`), ConvergenceBound (`checkConvergenceBound_sound`), Termination (`checkTermination_sound`), ParametricContraction (`checkParametric_sound`) and Estimate (`checkHoeffding_sound`) (`coverage.csv: kind / Mass` to `kind / Estimate`). None is computation only. For Estimate the theorem covers the sample count, not the stated value.
- There are 0 `sorry` and 0 declared axioms in the 15 Lean files of `lean/` (`coverage.csv: project / lean`) and in the 7 of `lean-engine/` (`coverage.csv: project / lean-engine`). The test `the_repository_has_no_sorry_and_every_kind_and_kernel_function_proved` checks both counts and the allowed axioms.
- Of the 49 functions in `axiom-core`, 4 are proved: `kernel::complement`, `kernel::contraction_factor`, `kernel::steady_state` and `kernel::affine_apply` (`coverage.csv: function / kernel::complement` and the three rows after it). Four public functions delegate to them, but the conversions around those calls are not translated: `Probability::complement`, `AffineUpdate::apply`, `TwoStateChain::contraction_factor` and `TwoStateChain::steady_state`. The 8 methods of `ExactRational` are modelled as `Rat` and trusted. The other 33 have no Lean theorem, among them the step count `TwoStateChain::steps_within` and all of `hoeffding.rs` (`coverage.csv: function / two_state_chain::TwoStateChain::steps_within` and `function / hoeffding::hoeffding_holds`).

## The two certainty levels

Every accepted claim carries one of two certainty levels, and `axiom check` prints it.

**Proven** covers every kind except Estimate. The claim follows from the model by `check_sound`, for every step and, for ParametricContraction, for every parameter in the intervals. It holds of the model at steps and parameters no simulation tried. In the demo, 6 of Alice's 8 claims are proven (`lean_demo_accepts_true_claims_and_rejects_negatives_and_mutants`).

**Estimated** covers the Estimate kind. The checker verifies only the arithmetic: that the sample count meets Hoeffding's bound, `2 exp(-2 n ε²) ≤ δ`, for the stated tolerance `ε` and failure probability `δ` (`checkHoeffding_sound`). It does not verify the value, the sampling, the random number generator or the independence of runs. At tolerance 1/100 and failure probability 1/1000 the bound needs 38,005 samples (`comparison.csv: tolerance-2 / ConvergenceBound`, column `sim_samples`). A mutant that states 100 samples where the demo's settings need 265 is rejected (`lean_demo_accepts_true_claims_and_rejects_negatives_and_mutants`). Black-box analysis produces only Estimated claims unless a candidate is afterwards proven from a model.

## Limitations

- **Model, not system.** An accepted claim is true of the model in the certificate. This project does not check whether the model describes Alice's real network.
- **Checking is not cheaper in general.** On the fixtures it was much cheaper only for ParametricContraction. Elsewhere it was cheaper only at tolerances of 1/10,000 or tighter, or when the chain mixes slowly. The fixed start-up cost of the Lean checker dominates small certificates.
- **Narrow model class.** FixedPoint, Contraction and ConvergenceBound are checked for two-state chains only, and larger chains receive Mass claims only. Termination covers the geometric retry model. A certificate that applies a chain claim to a retry model is refused as a whole (`checker-not-chain` in the demo).
- **Incomplete checker.** `check_sound` rules out accepting a false claim, but nothing proves that the checker accepts every true one. A rejection may come from the engine, a false claim, a format mismatch or a fault on the Lean side.
- **Partial implementation coverage.** Proof 3 covers 4 of 49 engine functions (`coverage.csv`, the `function` rows). The step count, the matrix construction and the Hoeffding computation in Rust are unproved, and the model of `ExactRational` as `Rat` is trusted. Proof 3 also trusts the pinned Charon and Aeneas.
- **Estimate quality is not measured.** No test measures the empirical coverage of Hoeffding intervals over repeated seeds. The guarantee rests on the inequality and on independent runs, which the checker cannot see.
- **Parser and semantics are trusted.** The JSON parser and the Lean statement of what each claim means are part of the TCB. A wrong statement would make `check_sound` prove the wrong thing.
- **Measurement noise.** The timings came from one machine and one run of `make bench`, keeping the median of 3 checks per certificate. Several simulation times are extrapolated from a prefix of the samples.
